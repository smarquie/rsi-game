Decentralized learning under a shared budget

Read the scope before interpreting a gap. Indefinite-world optima are best found by multi-start SLSQP, not certified global optima. The optional branch-and-bound command reports an explicit remaining gap. Finite-width targets need not be budget-feasible; overload may make raw outcomes incomparable with the feasible team benchmark. This report does not classify a short transient as a proven fixed point or cycle.

Design flags
Interpretation guide
    

  • Identification: exact curvature/slope recovery requires nonzero dither, the frequency conditions, a fixed free-function background, and a raw quadratic aggregate. Adaptive clearing and finite-horizon mixtures can break the fixed-background assumption.
    • 

  • Learning versus feasibility: raw Y, penalized reward, spending and overload are distinct series. A lower reward can reflect price instability or a infeasible exploration floor even if the fitted quadratic is exact.
    • 

  • Traps: nearest stationary candidate is a distance diagnostic, not proof of basin membership. Use longer runs, residual checks, multiple starts and the two-dimensional basin command.
    • 

  • Convergence: tail target range and settling time are finite-horizon diagnostics. Fixed-multiplier idealized tests differ from the finite-width, changing-price process.
    • 

  • Outside changes: the invisible construction preserves slices through the chosen target. It need not preserve observations under finite-width commitments, shifted boundary centers, or changing backgrounds.
    • 

  • Statistical unit: the baseline is deterministic conditional on the world. Changing only a seed does not create independent evidence. Random schedules and outside interventions supply nested randomness when enabled.
    •
